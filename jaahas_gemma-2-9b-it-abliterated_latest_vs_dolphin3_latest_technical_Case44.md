This is a fascinating and complex set of questions that delve into the heart of emerging AI governance and societal structures. Let's break down each component and explore potential quantitative answers:

**1. Causality Assignment and Liability in Autonomous AI**

* **Strict Liability Analogy:**  Drawing a parallel to canine ownership liability, we can envision a "strict liability" framework for autonomous AI. If an AI agent, acting within its programmed parameters, causes harm, the owner/operator (analogous to the dog owner) would be liable, regardless of negligence.

* **Tipping Point:**  Determining the precise tipping point where user-space operator control diminishes is complex. It depends on factors like:
    * **Agent Autonomy:**  How much decision-making power does the agent possess?
    * **Transparency:** Can we understand the agent's reasoning process and path generation?
    * **External Influences:**  Are there external factors (data, environment) significantly impacting the agent's actions?

* **Stochasticity and Degradation:**  Stochasticity (randomness) in open-weights AI introduces uncertainty.  We can model liability degradation using a non-linear function:

    * **Liability(t) = L0 * (1 - α^t)** 

    Where:
        * L0 = Initial liability
        * α =  A decay factor reflecting the impact of stochasticity and time (0 < α < 1)
        * t = Time

* **Traceability:**  Tokenized execution lines can be traced back to the physical workstation core using cryptographic hashes and blockchain-like structures. This requires:
    * **Unique Tokenization:** Each AI action generates a unique token.
    * **Immutable Ledger:** A distributed ledger records token provenance and execution history.
    * **Hardware Anchoring:**  Physical workstation hardware (e.g., unique MAC address) is linked to the initial token generation.

**2.  Centralized vs. Localized AI Governance**

* **Algorithmic Enclosure:** Centralized cloud-based AI systems can enforce ideological compliance through:
    * **Real-time Semantic Filtering:**  AI algorithms filter data based on predefined criteria, potentially suppressing dissenting viewpoints.
    * **Telemetry Harvesting:**  Data on user interactions and preferences is collected and analyzed to shape content and influence behavior.

* **Resilience of Local Networks:**  Local, air-gapped networks with abliterated open weights offer resilience against:
    * **Network Scarcity:**  Data and computation are decentralized, reducing reliance on external infrastructure.
    * **Access Blockades:**  Local networks can operate independently, even if access to the wider internet is restricted.

* **Data Sovereignty and Autarky:**  Tokenized transaction barriers (pay-to-query) and local hardware parameters (VRAM, compute) define the boundaries of data sovereignty:
    * **Tokenized Access:**  Users pay to access specific data or computational resources, controlling data flow.
    * **Hardware Constraints:**  Local processing power and memory limit the scope of AI operations, promoting self-sufficiency.

**3.  Operational Perimeter of Offline Data Fortresses**

* **VRAM/Compute Constraints:**  The operational perimeter of an offline data fortress depends on the specific AI tasks and the available hardware resources. 
    * **VRAM:**  Larger models require more VRAM.
    * **Compute:**  Complex computations demand more processing power.

* **Self-Sustaining Offline Operation:**  To achieve self-sustainability, offline data fortresses need:
    * **Local Data Storage:**  Sufficient capacity to store training data and model weights.
    * **Energy Efficiency:**  Low-power hardware and energy-saving techniques are crucial for long-term operation.
    * **Data Replication:**  Redundant data storage ensures resilience against hardware failures.



**Key Takeaways:**

* As AI autonomy increases, liability frameworks must evolve to account for the complexities of decision-making in stochastic systems.
* Decentralized, localized AI architectures offer potential solutions to algorithmic enclosure and data sovereignty concerns.
* Establishing robust traceability mechanisms and defining clear operational boundaries are essential for navigating the evolving landscape of AI governance.
